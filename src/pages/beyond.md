<h2 id="baku">From Baku to Berkeley</h2>

I grew up in Baku, Azerbaijan, around PlayStations and Windows 98 machines. I wanted to know what was going on inside them. At about fourteen, once my English was good enough to follow along, I learned to code from [Pamela Fox](https://pamelafox.org/)’s tutorials on [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org/).

After my A-levels in Oxford, I moved to California for Berkeley. On the first day of [CS 61A](https://cs61a.org/), I recognized the instructor’s voice before I placed her face. It was Pamela Fox, whose videos I’d watched on my Windows XP laptop in Baku. The move felt a little less daunting after that.

At Berkeley I got to meet, and sometimes take classes from, people I’d followed from afar, including [David Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Patterson_(computer_scientist)), [Ion Stoica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Stoica), [Matei Zaharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matei_Zaharia), [Paul Vixie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Vixie), [Stuart Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_J._Russell), and [Richard Karp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_M._Karp). I went to London for my master’s and now spend time there and in San Francisco.

## Teaching

I was a teaching assistant for ENGIN 125 (Ethics, Engineering and Society). For three terms I taught data structures in CS 61B with [Computer Science Mentors](https://csmentors.studentorg.berkeley.edu/#/), first as a junior mentor and then as a senior mentor. I also tutored CS 61B and CS 161 (Computer Security) for the EECS department, and CS 161 for [SEED Scholars](https://seedscholars.berkeley.edu/). Teaching the same material to different groups shaped the [assessment tools](/research/#teaching) I built at Berkeley.

## Open source

Alongside the [PrairieLearn graph assessments](https://github.com/eldarhasanov079/pl-interactive-graph-v2) and [graph constructor](https://github.com/eldarhasanov079/pl-graph-constructor), a few smaller projects live on GitHub.

- A [coverage-guided DIMACS SAT fuzzer](https://github.com/eldarhasanov079/dimacs-sat-fuzzer) for studying solver reliability.
- An [encrypted file-sharing client](https://github.com/eldarhasanov079/secure-file-sharing-go) in Go with invitations and revocation.
- A [RISC-V inference engine](https://github.com/eldarhasanov079/riscv-assembly-inference-engine) that implements the matrix operations in assembly.

## Hackathons

I’ve competed in more than fifteen hackathons, including CalHacks, TreeHacks, and SFHacks, and kept going after moving to London. [Clusy](/clusy/) started at one. Our projects placed first at IDDA Hackathon 2 and CulTech, won a technical execution award at Imperial, and finished in the top five of 155 teams at the UK Agentic AI Hackathon. I also placed third in both the Netcraft and Verno Labs security challenges. The [CV](/curriculum-vitae/#awards) has the projects and awards.
